Direct precedent
Detection of a Developing Hot Spot in Stored Corn with a CO₂ Sensor
Ileleji, Maier, Bhat & Woloshuk · Applied Engineering in Agriculture 22(2):275–289, 2006 · ASABE
Induced hot spot in a 12.5 t bin. Headspace CO₂ rose from 500 to 1,500–2,300 ppm across three trials. CO₂ sensors “detected spoilage earlier than temperature cables” — and “temperature cables alone might not be a reliable indicator of stored grain conditions.”
Real-time CO₂ monitoring for early detection of grain spoilage and mycotoxin contamination
Kiaitsi, Zanardi, Sulyok, García-Cela et al. · J. Sci. Food Agric., 2025
Mini-silos of naturally contaminated wheat at 15–30 % moisture (aw 0.78–0.98), comparing CO₂, temperature and RH as indicators of mould activity and mycotoxin contamination.
Validates our sensing triad
Integrated approach on stored grain quality management with CO₂ monitoring — A review
Ramachandran · J. Stored Products Research 96:101950, 2022 · 122 refs
Review establishing CO₂ monitoring as an accepted approach in stored grain management — not a novel or speculative signal.
Establishes the field
Modelling the effect of temperature and water activity on growth rate of A. flavus and aflatoxin production
Norlia, Jinap, Nor-Khaizura et al. · Int. J. Food Microbiology 335:108836, 2020
Published modelling of growth rate against temperature and water activity — the same cardinal-value approach our days-to-onset forecast uses.
Backs our growth model
Aflatoxin B₁ contamination in maize in Europe increases due to climate change
Battilani, Toscano, Van der Fels-Klerx et al. · Scientific Reports 6:24328, 2016 · 625 citations
Aflatoxins have the highest acute and chronic toxicity of all mycotoxins, and the risk in maize is rising with climate.
Why maize, why now
CO₂ detecting spoilage before temperature is established, published science. Our contribution is reaching it at depth, in bag stacks, at PACS cost.

Mohit — three claims to be able to defend

M1

The stability condition

The solver is explicit finite-difference with zero-flux boundaries. It is only stable while D·dt/dx² ≤ 0.25 — currently 0.18. Understand why the limit exists and what breaks past it. If anyone ever sees NaN on screen, this is the first thing to check.

M2

The coupling

A single respiration term drives both CO₂ production and heat release. They are not independent effects — they are the same biology observed two ways. This is the core of the model.

M3

Check the cap

Respiration is capped at 4,000 mg/kg/day to stop runaway self-heating. Verify it is never reached during a normal run. If it is, the cap is doing physics rather than protecting us from it, and that is a problem.

Vishal — four claims, two of them recently changed

V1

The noise model

Every reading gets Gaussian noise and quantization: CO₂ ±25 ppm + 1.5 %, RH ±1.0 %, temperature ±0.15 °C. Check these against real datasheets for the parts in our budget. “Our detector already runs on noisy readings, here is the noise model” is a far stronger answer than silence.

V2

Depth-matched comparison — changed

CO₂ is compared across lances at the same depth, not against all nine ports. Depth stratification is real and about 1,000 ppm; comparing all nine buried the signal completely. This is the change most likely to be questioned.

V3

Alert threshold — changed

Fires at 3.0 σ sustained over 3 consecutive readings, down from 4.0. Measured separation: clean grain peaks at 1.5 σ, a real hotspot reaches 4.2 σ. Verify this yourself — run the demo on clean grain for 60 days and confirm it stays silent.

1 · Sign off the physical constants — by Sunday 12:00

Verification Sheet pages 6–8, twelve items: stack dimensions, cell size, the three diffusivities, bulk density, porosity, specific heat, ambient conditions, probe geometry.

The question you will be asked

“Is your moisture figure a molecular diffusivity?” — No. It is an effective coefficient lumping convective moisture migration together with diffusion, because in a real bag stack convection dominates. Molecular diffusion alone would be far slower. Say this in your own words.

Get the SCD30. A sealed jar reads tens of thousands of ppm and a genuinely spoiling stack interior does too. A 5,000 ppm ceiling pegs and tells you nothing. It also returns humidity and temperature on the same chip, cutting part count.

Disable Automatic Baseline Correction — this will cost you a day

Every NDIR sensor assumes it sees fresh 400 ppm air every 24 hours and silently re-zeros to the lowest value it has seen. Inside grain it never sees 400 ppm, so ABC quietly recalibrates the elevated reading back down to “normal” and erases the exact signal we are measuring. Turn ABC off in firmware. This is the single most common way CO₂ grain-monitoring projects fail.

Not DHT22 — it drifts, it is slow, and it fails in exactly the condensing high-humidity conditions our own proposal names as a known failure mode. The SHT31 carries an onboard heater to burn off condensation, which is the mitigation we already promised in writing.

Accuracy matters more here than anywhere else: relative humidity is water activity. ±2 % RH becomes ±0.02 aw, and we distinguish 0.82 from 0.85.

The part everyone forgets — I²C address collision

The SHT31 has only two possible addresses (0x44 and 0x45, set by the ADDR pin). We want three, at three depths. Three on one bus collide and it will not work.

Fix: a TCA9548A I²C multiplexer, about ₹250. Eight channels, solves it completely, scales straight to nine sensors for the full three-lance array. Buy it now or rediscover this at two in the morning.

5 · Compatibility — read before wiring

Everything above is 3.3 V I²C, so no level shifters are needed

This is deliberate. ESP32 GPIO is not 5 V tolerant, and mixing 5 V parts onto it is how boards get killed. An all-3.3 V I²C set removes that entire class of problem.

One exception, the MH-Z19B if you end up with one: power it from 5 V, but its UART lines are 3.3 V, so TX and RX connect to the ESP32 directly. Do not run it on 3.3 V power — the infrared lamp will not drive properly and readings drift low.

Second gotcha: the SCD30 stretches the I²C clock up to 150 ms. ESP32 handles it, but you may need to raise Wire.setTimeOut() or reads return garbage.

I²C over 2 m is out of specification

Probe lances put sensors metres from the controller; I²C is designed for on-board distances. In order: drop the bus to 100 kHz, use 2.2 kΩ pull-ups instead of 4.7 kΩ, use shielded twisted cable. If it is still unreliable at full length, the real fix is a small microcontroller at each depth talking RS-485 — but do not design for that until the problem is measured.
